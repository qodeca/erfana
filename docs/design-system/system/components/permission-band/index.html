Permission band

decided reviewed 2026-10-28

A preview page that asks for a remote host gets one row here, not a toast. The chip opens the list; Allow opens a confirm step on that row; Confirm or Cancel ends it, and Esc closes. Every one of those states is drawn below as its own frame. The bar also holds an elastic load status slot for a page that is slow to arrive, and a tool that opens a panel stays pressed for as long as the panel is open.

What this card decides: the band is always present, and it is a flow sibling above the page area rather than an overlay on it. That placement is the only thing left separating Erfana's chrome from untrusted content. Conditional rendering or position: absolute would remove the last of it.

Why it replaces the toast. Approval used to be a pop-up per host, capped at three. A page reaching four hosts produced three stacked walls of identical text over the file tree – and the fourth host could not be approved at all. The band lists every host, in the preview's own chrome, and each decision is read once on the row it belongs to. How the card reached this is under History.

Every state, as the band renders it

Each frame is a snapshot of the band built from the shipping band.css and nav.css, one state per frame. Frames are pictures: nothing in them takes focus. The keyboard contract is the table below.

What an approval says back, and a list that stopped growing

A failed or late approval puts its one message in the row's 72px middle track (PreviewBandRow.tsx:53), and the band says it again in its polite region. Plain “Not saved” is frame D above; these are the reasoned variants that ship, then the notice for a truncated list. Each frame uses the shipping band.css at the app's scale, so text that does not fit the track is drawn not fitting. The app ships every dash in these strings as an em dash; no page here may carry that character, so the frames draw an en dash – slightly narrower – and the captions write ⟨em dash⟩. See Open defects.

The toolbar row: Back leads, chip trails

Issue #124 put two controls in front of Find, because they act on this tab – where its history goes, and what a plain link does in it – while Find acts on the page. Back leads the row, as it does in every browser, in Finder and in Explorer; the link-mode toggle follows it; an 8px gap, not a second rule, separates the pair from Find. The spacer still pushes the chip to the trailing edge, so the rule this card used to state as "Find leads, chip trails" is now "Back leads, chip trails". Open in default browser sits behind the separator with Export to PDF: both act outside the page. There is no Forward button – Back's tooltip names the Forward key, and Back's aria-keyshortcuts carries the Back key.

Back, in the frames below. With an earlier page, its tooltip names that page. With none, it is aria-disabled, still focused, its tooltip says why, and the live region says "Showing overview.html." With focus anywhere in the panel, ⌘[ and ⌘] (macOS) or Alt+← and Alt+→ (Windows, Linux) step the tab, matched on the physical key. The toggle's two states and Open in default browser's busy state are drawn as their own frames.

The pressed state is a shape, not a colour (SC 1.4.1): the Rows and lists card's "selected" signature, a fill plus a 2px lime edge along the bottom. It is the one "on" look in the bar. It marks every on/off tool – the link-mode toggle and Comment mode, on aria-pressed – and a tool that opens a panel, the Comments list that opens the comments drawer, on aria-expanded for as long as the panel is open. An open panel is never drawn as a box, an outline or a fill of its own (owner decision, 2026-10-28): a reader scanning the bar sees one signature for "this is on". Both states carry the edge – transparent when off, with a matching transparent one on top – so the icon sits where every other tool's sits and never moves. It is a border, so it survives a Windows contrast theme, where a forced-colors rule turns it Highlight.

Contrast, derived: the icons, --color-text-primary on --color-bg-primary, are 11.51:1; the lime edge against the band is 13.94:1 – both clear of the 3:1 a control needs. On the pressed fill, composited over the band, the icon is 7.94:1 and the inset focus ring 5.79:1 – so the toggle keeps the band's inset ring.

A control that is dimmed still shows a full-strength focus ring. The dim lives on the icon, never on the button: opacity on the button makes a group that the focus outline is inside, and the ring came out at roughly 2.3:1 there – under the 3:1 SC 1.4.11 asks of a focus indicator, and under "visible" (SC 2.4.7). No ring colour repairs that, because the dim is applied after the colour. Outside the group it is the band's ordinary ring: 8.39:1 against --color-bg-primary. This is the common case rather than an edge one – the panel moves focus to Back after a move, which is the moment Back has nowhere to go. Tab to the disabled Back below: the arrow stays dim, the ring does not. Where a control's whole content is a bare text node there is no box to dim. So the confirm step's Confirm and the failed banner's buttons (not on this card) wrap their label in a span and take this same shape: focused and busy, they still read as busy.

Shortcuts are captions, not tooltips

A shortcut that lives only in a title is invisible to most of the people who need it. A title appears on mouse hover only: not on keyboard focus, not on touch, and a screen reader may or may not read it. So the band's tools carry their keys twice. Visibly, as a caption under the tool's name in the tooltip the band draws on hover and on keyboard focus, in the key style. Programmatically, as aria-keyshortcuts on the button. The title keeps the name only – and Back's name says which page it goes to, because that changes.
Decided here, 2026-10-28: the Comments list wears the pressed signature while the drawer is open – drawn above, beside the link-mode toggle. The rest of the comment tools stays proposed.

Decided, 2026-10-29 (spec 024 C-15, C-20; D-2): two comment tools after Find – Comment mode and the Comments list, the labelled count "7 comments ▲1", in this card's tool and toggle shapes. Show comment icons left the row: it is the comments drawer's checkbox. The tools make every tier wider, so the thresholds below must be re-measured before they ship; the Comment tools in the toolbar card renders the row with them and prints what each tier then needs. This card changes when that one is promoted.

Load status slot: still loading, stopped, Reload

Decided by the owner, 2026-10-28. A page that is slow to arrive is not an error, but it must not look like a finished page either. So the bar gets one elastic status slot, between the spacer and the chip, that says one of two things:
  • Still loading – “Still loading – part of the page has not arrived”, in --color-text-tertiary, 8.52:1 on the band.
  • Stopped – “Stopped. The page may be missing parts.”, in --color-warning (8.00:1 · pass on the band) with the warning icon in front, so colour is never the only signal (SC 1.4.1). Reload follows the line.
The slot follows the Toolbar card's .erf-toolbar__status: it shrinks and ends in an ellipsis before any control gives way, and it never wraps – a taller bar would push the native page down, a bounds push and a fresh proof cycle, and a slow load is not worth that. That is also why it never carries a host name: a host is never cut (Rows and lists card). Below about 420px of bar width the line drops out; near there it gets room for an ellipsis and nothing a person could read. Reload stays at every width – it is the way back.

Reload is an existing control, not a new one: the band's tool button, the Toolbar and Buttons cards' icon tool as it ships in this bar (.erf-band__tool, 32 × 28, the band's inset focus ring), with the registry's refresh icon, the accessible name “Reload” and the tooltip “Reload”. It has no shortcut, so it has no aria-keyshortcuts and no key caption. No warning fill: the line before it carries the warning.

When the slot shows – the show delay and the point at which a wait becomes “stopped” – is the Waiting for a page screen's call, still proposed. This card decides where the words go, what they say, how they shrink and how they are announced.

Narrow panels: the row never wraps

A taller band pushes the native page down – a bounds push and the 300 ms proof cycle – so the row never wraps and never changes height. It steps down instead, by a container query on .erf-band. Compact, below the width the full row needs: tighter padding and gaps, and the chip keeps "12 blocked" but drops "· 34 allowed", so what stays visible is still the start of its accessible name (SC 2.5.3). Narrow, below the width the compact row needs: Export to PDF leaves the row and the Tab order, and Find stays. Minimal, below the width the narrow row needs: Find leaves too. Both shortcuts keep working with focus in the page, which is reachable by keyboard; Open in default browser stays, because it has none. Anything that still does not fit scrolls sideways with no visible scrollbar, and focus brings a control into view (SC 2.4.11).

The thresholds in nav.css are literal px, and they are measured here – a custom property cannot be used in a container condition. Every band below carries a two-digit chip and prints what its row at a width inside each tier; a tier's threshold is the need of the tier above it. Change a tool, a gap or the chip, and read these numbers again before touching the CSS.

Today the tiers switch below 534px, 446px and 378px (read from nav.css). Measure a row's need in the browser's inspector before changing them.

The unit is an origin, not a hostname

A row grants scheme, host and port – because that is the unit both chokepoints can compare. A hostname could not carry it. A CSP host-source with no port matches only the scheme's default port, while the network filter compared bare hostnames and ignored the port entirely, so the two gates were answering different questions about the same request.

That mismatch was visible, and it was the worst kind of visible. Approving example.com and then loading https://example.com:8443/x.js got the resource refused by the CSP – while the band, which subtracted every allowed host from the blocked list, dropped the row and reported example.com – Allowed ✓. A permanently broken resource, labelled as working, with no control anywhere that could fix it. Both gates now compare one re-serialised origin, so the two cannot disagree.

But an origin printed in full is not scannable, and a row nobody reads is not a control. So the host stays prominent and the other two parts are drawn only when they are not the default – the scheme when it is not https, the port when it is not the scheme's own. The common case is unchanged; anything unusual is visible because it is unusual.

De-emphasised is not hidden. The scheme and port use --color-text-tertiary, 8.52:1 on the band's --color-bg-primary, – quieter than the host name, still far clear of the 4.5:1 floor. (--color-text-secondary would also pass here, at 5.00:1; the 3.72:1 failure this card once quoted was measured on --color-bg-secondary, a surface the band no longer uses.) A part of a security decision may be quietened, not made hard to read. And the accessible name carries the full origin whether or not it is drawn, so a screen-reader user and a sighted user are answering the same question.

What the confirm step has to say, and why

No row names a resource kind – not a blocked one, not an allowed one. An earlier draft of this card put "script" or "image" beside every blocked host. The word was true and the placement made it false: next to a host and an Allow button it reads as the scope of the block and of the grant, and neither is scoped. previewCsp.ts appends the same host list to script-src, style-src, img-src, font-src, media-src and connect-src – so a host is refused for everything, and once approved it is allowed for everything.

The kind belongs in exactly one place: the confirm box, where the very next clause takes the limit back. "It was blocked for a stylesheet, but Erfana cannot limit it to that – the host will also be able to run code and send data to it." A word that misleads on a row is safe in a sentence that contradicts it. There is no such sentence on a row.

The confirm step also says the grant is written into the project, travels to anyone who clones the repository, and cannot be undone from inside Erfana. That is the honest sentence – and it is read once, on the row you chose, instead of four times over your file tree.

The data agrees with the decision. PreviewAllowlistSchema stores hosts and nothing else, so for a host approved last month, or one that arrived with a cloned repository, the kind is unknowable. A column that could only ever be filled for this session's blocks would invite the reader to infer a difference in grant that does not exist.

If #81 ever scopes a grant to the kinds it was refused for, the column earns its place back – but only then, and the card is the thing to change first.

The confirm step's focus ring goes outward

Confirm is filled with --color-accent-primary, and --color-border-focus is the same colour. The band draws its focus rings inside the control – a ring on a row's Allow would be clipped by the host list's scroller – and inside this button means on top of its own fill: 1.00:1. Not dim, not subtle: absent, on the most irreversible control in the app (SC 2.4.7). It was invisible whether or not the button was busy, so it is a second defect rather than part of the dimming one above.

Decided: the filled variant alone takes an outward ring, at the chip's offset, so it lands on the confirm block's own surface – 6.95:1 against --color-bg-tertiary, clear of the 3:1 a focus indicator needs. Every other .erf-band__allow keeps the inset ring: those are transparent, so their ring never lands on a fill. This button is inside the scroller too, but it sits within the confirm block's 16px padding and the ring needs 4px of it, so the clip cannot reach it – keep that padding if the rule moves.

A second ring colour for one button was the alternative and was rejected: one ring colour is the Focus and keyboard card's decision, and a per-variant exception is a second rule to keep in step for a case that placement already solves.

The band's text contrast

The band's labels use --color-text-tertiary, which is 8.52:1 on the band's real surface, --color-bg-primary – clear of the 4.5:1 floor. --color-text-secondary would also pass, at 5.00:1, so the tertiary choice currently has no stated reason. It is kept for now, and re-deciding it is open work rather than a settled rule.

The one warning line – an unencrypted origin, in the confirm box – uses --color-warning at 6.63:1 against --color-bg-tertiary, the confirm block's own surface rather than the band's, also clear of the floor. It is distinguished rather than louder: the whole box is a security question, and colouring one line inside it would suggest the other lines are safe.

A pressed toggle – the link mode, on – sits on --color-bg-selected over the band. Its icon measures 7.94:1 on that fill and its focus ring 5.79:1, both measured after compositing the translucent fill over --color-bg-primary.

History

This card used to decide that the band carried a permanent “Preview – content below is not Erfana” label, and a 2px accent seam the page could not paint over. It no longer does. Both were removed by owner decision when the bar became a toolbar matching MarkdownToolbar, and the seam is now the same 1px neutral rule.

Recorded here rather than quietly deleted, because a decided card that changes its mind owes the next reader the reason. docs/design-system/README.md is explicit that this is allowed – “a decided card the app keeps having to deviate from is a card that was wrong” – but the cost is real and belongs on the card: nothing on screen names the boundary any more, and 1px of --color-border-default is weak against the white paper a page picks by default. docs/security.md residual risk 8 carries the accepted residual.

The contrast measurement was once wrong. This card used to say --color-text-secondary failed on the band. It does not. The failing figure was measured against --color-bg-secondary, borrowed from a claim that belongs to the Colours card, and left behind when the band moved to --color-bg-primary. Every gate stayed green throughout, because the number was right about the pair it named and the pair was not this one.

Open defects this card names

Where the app has not caught up with a decision on this card. Checked on 2026-10-28 against src/renderer/src/components/Panels/HtmlPreviewPanel/.

  • Load status slot – decided, not yet built. The bar ships no status slot: there is no “Still loading” or “Stopped” line and no Reload tool in PreviewChromeBand.tsx or PreviewNavControls.tsx, and neither string exists anywhere in the panel's source. Reload ships only in the failure banner (PreviewBanner.tsx, through usePreviewPanelActions.ts). Until it is built, a page that hangs halfway looks finished. The slot's CSS above is drawn on this card only, not synced from band.css; when the app builds it, the rules move into PreviewChromeBand.css and come back here through the sync.
  • The reasoned result messages do not fit their track. The middle track is sized to “Not saved” (the band.css deviation note says so), but four shipped variants are longer – “Not saved ⟨em dash⟩ list full”, “… not allowed”, “… check settings” and “Saved ⟨em dash⟩ reload to apply” (permissionBand.logic.ts:354–372). .erf-band__state is white-space: nowrap, so the text runs out of its 72px track, across the gap and under that row's Allow button: frames R1–R4 draw it. The logic's own comment says the message “must fit rather than widen the grid”; today it does neither.
  • “Saved – reload to apply” is drawn as a failure. On a timeout the grant is already saved, yet the row's text takes --color-error, the chip keeps its red caret and the row still offers Allow (frame R4). The words are right; the colour and the button say the opposite, so the reader is invited to retry a grant that exists.
  • The polite region drops the reason. Every “Not saved” variant announces the same sentence, “Could not save … to this project. It is still blocked.” (permissionBand.logic.ts:133), and focus goes to the chip, whose name carries only the counts. A sighted reader sees “list full” or “check settings”; a screen-reader user hears neither unless they browse to the row – not the same question, which this card's origin rule asks for.
  • The truncated-list notice can say something untrue, and is silent. Main sets the flag when the list reaches its cap (previewBlockedLedger.ts:158, a >= comparison), so a page that asked for exactly the cap's worth of hosts is told “this page asked for more”. The other way round (inferred from the code, not run), an origin dropped by the per-hostname sub-cap (MAX_BLOCKED_ORIGINS_PER_HOST) sends no event, so that loss never sets the flag. The notice is a section label, drawn in uppercase and not announced (frame T).
  • Em dashes in the shipped strings. All five strings in frames R1–R4 and T ship with an em dash where the Writing card asks for an en dash; that card lists them with file and line. This card draws them with an en dash, because no page here may carry the character.

The pressed signature on an open panel's tool already ships: PreviewNavControls.css gives .erf-band__tool--toggle[aria-expanded='true'] the same fill and lime edge as aria-pressed, with the same forced-colors edge, and CommentToolbarTools.tsx puts that class on the Comments list. No row is needed for it.
