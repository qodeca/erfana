Erfana design system / Components / Feedback

Feedback

proposed reviewed 2026-08-31

How Erfana says that worked, that failed, this needs you or still working: the semantic severity tokens and nothing else; one banner shape whose severity is spoken as a word, boxed inside a dialog or window and as a full-width band across a panel; a toast that never takes focus and never times out on an error; a progress bar that always has a name; and a meter that states its level in real units. Everything is linkable from feedback.css.

Decision one – which amber is "warning"

Erfana declares 7 tokens for the meaning "warning" and 7 for "error". The toast – the surface users see most – is the odd one out, using an icon tint rather than the warning colour. The semantic tokens win. Everything else is retired for this purpose, and the retired ones now say so in design-tokens.css.

Those two numbers count declared tokens, and the basis matters. This card previously said "four warning, three error" with no basis given – it was both wrong and unfalsifiable, because nobody could tell whether it meant tokens, literal values, or foreground hues. A count with no stated denominator is not evidence.

Open defect: success and warning are both yellow

--color-success is the brand lime and --color-warning an amber-yellow. Side by side the two measure only 1.74:1 apart, and with the hue taken away – the right-hand pair below, in greyscale, as a reader with a colour-vision deficiency or a washed-out screen gets it – the two banners are the same banner.

Decision two – one banner, in two forms

Four severities, one shape: an icon, a title, a sentence, and – when there is something the reader can do – a compact row of actions on the end edge. Erfana spells a banner 3 different ways in its stylesheets (distinct banner class families, already diverged), and the comment panel's banner alone has 5 variants.

The severity is spoken as a word, not only shown as a colour. The icon is aria-hidden and the tint is invisible to a screen reader, so every banner, band and toast title starts with a visually hidden word – Warning:, Error:, Note: or Done: – and a screen-reader user hears what a sighted user sees. The word lives in the title (or, in a band with no title, at the start of the sentence), not in an aria-label, so it is read in order and survives translation. A banner without it is a defect, even when its icon and colour are right.

The message is a state of the whole panel – the file was deleted, auto-refresh is off, the preview failed. It sits under the panel's toolbar, full width, and has the Permission band as its sibling. Decision three – telling a screen reader

Erfana has four incompatible strategies for this, and three of them are unreliable. role="alert" alone appears in 12 files – the most-used of the four, and the one whose own comments call it unreliable. Only one is correct, and it is already in the toast module.
ApproachVerdict
A hidden region that is always mounted and empty until there is news This one. The browser watches the region for changes. It has to exist before the text arrives.
role="alert" on a box that appears at the same moment as its text Unreliable. There was nothing to watch until the moment it changed. Erfana's own code comments say so, yet role="alert" appears in 12 files, and not every one of them is mounted before its text.
Move focus and hope aria-describedby gets read Works only when focus genuinely should move. Not an announcement mechanism.
A region that unmounts when idle Same failure as the second. It does not exist until it matters.
Politeness. aria-live="polite" for everything – it waits for a pause. assertive interrupts whatever the person is reading, so it is for errors only.

Reserve the height. A message appearing in an empty region shoves the page down. The crash screen reserves the space for its message for exactly this reason, and nothing else does.

Toast

A toast reports something that has finished. It sits in a corner, it never takes focus, and it leaves on its own only when missing it costs nothing. The rules below are what toastDwell.ts, toastStack.ts and ToastNotification.tsx already enforce; the card adopts them and fixes the toast's colours.

Toast, or banner?

Use a toast whenUse a banner when
It is finished and needs no reply – "Exported to PDF". It may vanish because nothing is lost if it is missed. It is about the thing on screen, or the reader has to decide something. It stays until the situation changes.
A permission is never a toast. Approving a remote host is a decision with consequences that outlive the message, so it belongs on the surface it affects – see the Permission band card. This card exists partly because that rule was learned the hard way.

Progress

A bar for work that takes more than a moment and happens inside a dialog or panel the reader is watching. 2 files ship one today (the transcription and document-import dialogs). A short wait inside a control is the Motion card's spinner, not a bar.

Two names, two things. .erf-progress is the spinner – inline “still working” with no amount, owned by the States pattern (states.css). .erf-progress-bar (with __label, __value, __track, __fill, --indeterminate) is the bar drawn here, in feedback.css. The bar used to share the spinner's name; the two could not be loaded on one page. 
<!-- determinate: a stable name, the value in aria-valuenow -->
<div role="progressbar" aria-label="Import progress"
     aria-valuemin="0" aria-valuemax="100" aria-valuenow="45"
     aria-valuetext="45%, page 9 of 20">…</div>

<!-- indeterminate: the same name, and NO aria-valuenow -->
<div role="progressbar" aria-label="Import progress"
     aria-valuetext="Starting import">…</div>
RuleWhy
role="progressbar" with an aria-label that never changesA progress bar must have an accessible name. Putting the percentage in the name makes the name change on every tick; the value belongs in aria-valuenow.
Determinate: aria-valuemin, aria-valuemax, aria-valuenowAssistive tech reads the value on request, without a live region shouting every percent.
Indeterminate: no aria-valuenowIts absence is what tells assistive tech the amount is unknown.
Only start and finish are announcedThrough the always-mounted live region of decision three – never once per percent.
Reduced motion stops the sweepAnd replaces it with a still fill that cannot be mistaken for a value.
The fill is --color-accent-primary6.95:1 · pass against the track, the 3:1 a graphic needs (WCAG 1.4.11).

Meter

A meter shows a level inside a known range – how full the Claude Code context window is – not work moving toward done. It is role="meter", never progressbar: a progress bar promises that it will finish, a meter does not. The reference is the terminal's context meter, ClaudeStatusBar.tsx (the role="meter" element, lines 129–134) and ClaudeStatusBar.css (lines 62–104).

<div class="erf-meter erf-meter--warning">
  <div class="erf-meter__track" role="meter" aria-label="Claude Code context usage"
       aria-valuemin="0" aria-valuemax="100" aria-valuenow="74"
       aria-valuetext="Opus, 74% used, 148k of 200k tokens">
    <div class="erf-meter__fill" style="width: 74%"></div>
  </div>
  <span class="erf-meter__value">74%</span>
</div>
RuleWhy
role="meter" with a stable name (aria-label) and aria-valuemin, aria-valuemax, aria-valuenowThe name says what is measured and never changes; the value lives in the value attributes, as for the progress bar.
aria-valuetext in real units“74% used, 148k of 200k tokens” – the reader hears the amount, not only a percentage of an unnamed whole. The name is not repeated inside it.
Each band is carried by the visible number as well as the colourWCAG 1.4.1: colour is never the only signal. The value text takes the band colour, so it repeats the band rather than replacing it.
Track --color-bg-tertiary, --space-2 high, no borderA thin rule that sits in a status bar without crowding its text.
Fill colours come from the Colours cardSafe, warning and critical are decided once, with their contrast, at Colours – context meter. This card links to them rather than copying them, so there is one place to change.
The width moves with --transition-normal, and stops moving under reduced motionThe value changes in steps; the motion only eases them, so removing it loses nothing.
Not announced on every changeThe meter is read on request. A level crossing into critical is worth a polite announcement through decision three's region – once, not per token.

Open defects this card names

  • Success and warning are both yellow. See decision one; the shape and the word carry the difference until the palette changes.
  • The comment window's banner has no spoken severity word. CommentBanner (CommentParts.tsx, lines 89–110) renders the icon and the title but no visually hidden “Warning:” / “Error:” / “Note:” / “Done:” before it, so a screen-reader user hears the sentence without its severity. App follow-up: prefix the title (or the text, when there is no title) per kind. App code is not changed by this card.
  • No info tone yet. --color-info-bg and --color-info-border do not exist in design-tokens.css; .erf-banner--info draws them from a fallback mix, and the comment window's info banner borrows the accent (see Exceptions).
  • The band strips read retired tokens. .deletedBanner and .file-deleted-warning paint their text in --color-error-text-light and --color-warning-icon; the adoption table names the replacement.
  • The context meter reads retired tokens. ClaudeStatusBar.css paints amber and red with --color-warning-bright and --color-error-bright; the Colours card decides --color-warning and --color-error.
