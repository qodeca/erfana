Permission band

A preview page that asks for a remote host gets one row here, not a toast. Click the chip to open it. Click Allow, read the confirm step, then Confirm or Cancel. Press Escape to close.

This card used to decide that the band carried a permanent “Preview — content below is not Erfana” label, and a 2px accent seam the page could not paint over. It no longer does. Both were removed by owner decision when the bar became a toolbar matching MarkdownToolbar, and the seam is now the same 1px neutral rule.

Recorded here rather than quietly deleted, because a decided card that changes its mind owes the next reader the reason. design/README.md is explicit that this is allowed — “a decided card the app keeps having to deviate from is a card that was wrong” — but the cost is real and belongs on the card: nothing on screen names the boundary any more, and 1px of --color-border-default is weak against the white paper a page picks by default. docs/security.md residual risk 8 carries the accepted residual.

What this card still decides, and it is now the whole of it: the band is always present, and it is a flow sibling above the page area rather than an overlay on it. That placement is the only thing left separating Erfana's chrome from untrusted content. Conditional rendering or position: absolute would remove the last of it.
The band's own text is measured, not assumed — and the measurement was wrong. Its labels use --color-text-tertiary, which is :1 on the band's real surface, --color-bg-primary — clear of the 4.5:1 floor.

This card used to say --color-text-secondary failed here. It does not: :1 against the same surface, which passes. The failing figure was measured against --color-bg-secondary, borrowed from a claim that belongs to the colours foundation, and left behind when the band moved to --color-bg-primary. Every gate stayed green throughout, because the number was right about the pair it named and the pair was not this one. So the tertiary choice currently has no stated reason. It is kept for now, and re-deciding it is open work rather than a settled rule.

The one warning line — an unencrypted origin, in the confirm box — uses --color-warning at :1 against --color-bg-tertiary, the confirm block's own surface rather than the band's, also clear of the floor. It is distinguished rather than louder: the whole box is a security question, and colouring one line inside it would suggest the other lines are safe.
Why this replaces the toast. Approval used to be a pop-up per host, capped at three. A page reaching four hosts produced three stacked walls of identical text over the file tree — and the fourth host could not be approved at all. The band lists every host, in the preview's own chrome, and each decision is read once on the row it belongs to.

The toolbar row: Back leads, chip trails

Issue #124 put two controls in front of Find, because they act on this tab – where its history goes, and what a plain link does in it – while Find acts on the page. Back leads the row, as it does in every browser, in Finder and in Explorer; the link-mode toggle follows it; an 8px gap, not a second rule, separates the pair from Find. The spacer still pushes the chip to the trailing edge, so the rule this card used to state as "Find leads, chip trails" is now "Back leads, chip trails". Open in default browser sits behind the separator with Export to PDF: both act outside the page. There is no Forward button – Back's tooltip names the Forward key, and Back's aria-keyshortcuts carries the Back key.

Try it in the band above. Back steps a two-page history (overview.html, then pricing.html). One press and it is at the first page: aria-disabled, still focused, its tooltip saying why, and "Showing overview.html." in the live region. With focus anywhere in the panel, Cmd+[ and Cmd+] (macOS) or Alt+← and Alt+→ (Windows, Linux) step it too – the card takes both, matched on the physical key as the app does. The toggle flips its pressed state. Open in default browser goes busy for a moment and keeps focus.

The pressed state is a shape, not a colour (SC 1.4.1): the Rows card's "selected" signature, a fill plus a 2px lime edge along the bottom. Both states carry the edge – transparent when off, with a matching transparent one on top – so the icon sits where every other tool's sits and never moves. It is a border, so it survives a Windows contrast theme, where a forced-colors rule turns it Highlight.

Contrast, derived: the icons, --color-text-primary on --color-bg-primary, are :1; the lime edge against the band is :1 – both clear of the 3:1 a control needs. Not derived yet: the icon and the focus ring on the pressed fill. --color-bg-selected is translucent, and the ledger's contrast predicate reads opaque hex only, so it cannot composite the fill over the band. Those two pairs are checked at the walk-through; if the inset ring falls under 3:1 on the fill, the toggle's ring moves outward and gets an Exceptions row.

A control that is dimmed still shows a full-strength focus ring. The dim lives on the icon, never on the button: opacity on the button makes a group that the focus outline is inside, and the ring came out at roughly 2.3:1 there – under the 3:1 SC 1.4.11 asks of a focus indicator, and under "visible" (SC 2.4.7). No ring colour repairs that, because the dim is applied after the colour. Outside the group it is the band's ordinary ring: :1 against --color-bg-primary. This is the common case rather than an edge one – the panel moves focus to Back after a move, which is the moment Back has nowhere to go. Tab to the disabled Back below: the arrow stays dim, the ring does not. Where a control's whole content is a bare text node there is no box to dim. So the confirm step's Confirm and the failed banner's buttons (not on this card) wrap their label in a span and take this same shape: focused and busy, they still read as busy.

Narrow panels: the row never wraps

A taller band pushes the native page down – a bounds push and the 300 ms proof cycle – so the row never wraps and never changes height. It steps down instead, by a container query on .erf-band. Compact, below the width the full row needs: tighter padding and gaps, and the chip keeps "12 blocked" but drops "· 34 allowed", so what stays visible is still the start of its accessible name (SC 2.5.3). Minimal, below the width the compact row needs: Find and Export to PDF leave the row and the Tab order, while their shortcuts keep working with focus in the page, which is reachable by keyboard; Open in default browser stays, because it has none. Anything that still does not fit scrolls sideways with no visible scrollbar, and focus brings a control into view (SC 2.4.11).

The thresholds in nav.css are literal px, and they are measured here – a custom property cannot be used in a container condition. Every band below carries a two-digit chip and prints what its row needs at the tier it is in; a tier's threshold is the need of the tier above it. Change a tool, a gap or the chip, and read these numbers again before touching the CSS.

The unit is an origin, not a hostname

A row grants scheme, host and port — because that is the unit both chokepoints can compare. A hostname could not carry it. A CSP host-source with no port matches only the scheme's default port, while the network filter compared bare hostnames and ignored the port entirely, so the two gates were answering different questions about the same request.

That mismatch was visible, and it was the worst kind of visible. Approving example.com and then loading https://example.com:8443/x.js got the resource refused by the CSP — while the band, which subtracted every allowed host from the blocked list, dropped the row and reported example.com — Allowed ✓. A permanently broken resource, labelled as working, with no control anywhere that could fix it. Both gates now compare one re-serialised origin, so the two cannot disagree.

But an origin printed in full is not scannable, and a row nobody reads is not a control. So the host stays prominent and the other two parts are drawn only when they are not the default — the scheme when it is not https, the port when it is not the scheme's own. The common case is unchanged; anything unusual is visible because it is unusual.

De-emphasised is not hidden. The scheme and port use --color-text-tertiary, never --color-text-secondary, which measures 3.72:1 on this surface and fails AA — a part of a security decision may be quietened, not made hard to read. And the accessible name carries the full origin whether or not it is drawn, so a screen-reader user and a sighted user are answering the same question.

What the confirm step has to say, and why

No row names a resource kind — not a blocked one, not an allowed one. An earlier draft of this card put "script" or "image" beside every blocked host. The word was true and the placement made it false: next to a host and an Allow button it reads as the scope of the block and of the grant, and neither is scoped. previewCsp.ts appends the same host list to script-src, style-src, img-src, font-src, media-src and connect-src — so a host is refused for everything, and once approved it is allowed for everything.

The kind belongs in exactly one place: the confirm box, where the very next clause takes the limit back. "It was blocked for a stylesheet, but Erfana cannot limit it to that — the host will also be able to run code and send data to it." A word that misleads on a row is safe in a sentence that contradicts it. There is no such sentence on a row.

The confirm step also says the grant is written into the project, travels to anyone who clones the repository, and cannot be undone from inside Erfana. That is the honest sentence — and it is read once, on the row you chose, instead of four times over your file tree.

The data agrees with the decision. PreviewAllowlistSchema stores hosts and nothing else, so for a host approved last month, or one that arrived with a cloned repository, the kind is unknowable. A column that could only ever be filled for this session's blocks would invite the reader to infer a difference in grant that does not exist.

If #81 ever scopes a grant to the kinds it was refused for, the column earns its place back — but only then, and the card is the thing to change first.

The confirm step's focus ring goes outward

Confirm is filled with --color-accent-primary, and --color-border-focus is the same colour. The band draws its focus rings inside the control — a ring on a row's Allow would be clipped by the host list's scroller — and inside this button means on top of its own fill: :1. Not dim, not subtle: absent, on the most irreversible control in the app (SC 2.4.7). It was invisible whether or not the button was busy, so it is a second defect rather than part of the dimming one above.

Decided: the filled variant alone takes an outward ring, at the chip's offset, so it lands on the confirm block's own surface — :1 against --color-bg-tertiary, clear of the 3:1 a focus indicator needs. Every other .erf-band__allow keeps the inset ring: those are transparent, so their ring never lands on a fill. This button is inside the scroller too, but it sits within the confirm block's 16px padding and the ring needs 4px of it, so the clip cannot reach it — keep that padding if the rule moves.

A second ring colour for one button was the alternative and was rejected: one ring colour is the Focus foundation's decision, and a per-variant exception is a second rule to keep in step for a case that placement already solves.
